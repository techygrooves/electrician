Overview

Backup Generator Installation for your home

In Florida, storm season and the outages that come with it are a fact of life. A backup power plan can keep refrigeration, lighting, fans or air conditioning, medical equipment, and communications running when the grid goes down.

Bright Flow Services installs whole-home standby generators and appropriately configured transfer switch systems for portable generators. We help you decide what needs to stay powered, then plan an installation that connects backup power to your home the right way. If your panel needs attention first, we can plan that work alongside an electrical panel upgrade.

  • Manual transfer switches or interlock systems for portable generators
  • Generator power inlets for a safe portable generator connection
  • Load planning to decide which circuits stay powered

Signs it may be time to talk to an electrician

These are common reasons homeowners reach out. If any apply to you, we’ll help you understand your options.

  • 1Outages in your area have lasted hours or days
  • 2Someone in your home relies on powered medical equipment

  • 5You want to protect food, comfort, and security during storm season
  • 6You’re upgrading your panel and want to plan for backup power at the same time

Safety first. Never plug a generator into a wall outlet. This “backfeeding” can energize utility lines and seriously injure utility workers or others, and it can damage your home’s wiring. Always run portable generators outdoors, well away from doors, windows, and vents, to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning.

FAQ

Backup Generator Installation questions

Should I choose a standby generator or a portable generator?

A standby generator is permanently installed and can start automatically when the power goes out. A portable generator costs less up front but must be set up and started manually, and it usually powers a more limited set of circuits through a transfer switch or interlock. We’ll help you compare them for your home and priorities.

What size generator do I need?

It depends on what you want to run during an outage. Central air conditioning, well pumps, and electric ranges need far more capacity than lights and a refrigerator. We base our recommendation on the loads you choose to back up.

Why do I need a transfer switch?

A transfer switch or approved interlock separates your home from utility power before the generator supplies it. Without one, connecting a generator to your home’s wiring can backfeed utility lines, which is dangerous and can be deadly.

Is it safe to connect a generator with a double-ended cord?

No. Double-ended “suicide” cords are extremely dangerous and can electrocute you or utility workers. A properly installed inlet with a transfer switch or interlock is the safe way to connect a portable generator.

Do generators need maintenance?

Yes. Generators need periodic maintenance according to the manufacturer’s recommendations so they’re ready when you need them. Check your generator’s documentation for the recommended schedule.
