Overview

Emergency Troubleshooting & Repairs for Florida businesses

Electrical problems in a commercial building rarely happen at a convenient time. A tripping breaker can take down a line of equipment, flickering lights affect customers and staff, and a loss of power can stop business entirely.

Bright Flow Services diagnoses and repairs commercial electrical faults involving breakers, flickering lighting, failed panels, and loss of power. We trace problems systematically, explain what we find in plain terms, and recommend repairs that address the root cause. Contact us to describe the issue and discuss scheduling.

When to bring us in

Common situations where business owners, property managers, and contractors reach out.

  • 1Breakers trip repeatedly or won’t reset

  • 3A panel, breaker, or outlet is warm, buzzing, or discolored

Safety first. If you see smoke, sparking, or signs of burning, keep people away from the equipment and call 911 if there is any fire. Don’t open electrical panels or keep resetting a breaker that trips, because repeated resets can worsen a fault. Contact a qualified electrician to investigate.

FAQ

Emergency Troubleshooting & Repairs questions

How quickly can you respond to an electrical problem?

Availability depends on our schedule and the nature of the issue. Contact us to describe the problem and we’ll discuss the earliest available time. If there is smoke, fire, or immediate danger, call 911 first.

Why does a breaker keep tripping?

Common causes include overloaded circuits, short circuits, ground faults, failing equipment, or a worn breaker. Repeated tripping is a warning sign and should be investigated rather than repeatedly reset.

What causes lights to flicker throughout a building?

Building-wide flicker can point to loose connections, issues at the service or main distribution, or large loads starting and stopping. Localized flicker is more often a fixture, driver, or circuit issue. Diagnosis narrows it down.

Should we call the utility or an electrician when we lose power?

If neighboring properties are also out, contact the utility first. If the outage is limited to your building or part of it, the problem may be on your side of the meter and an electrician should investigate.

Can you help prevent future electrical failures?

After a repair, we can point out conditions that may lead to future problems, such as overloaded panels or aging equipment, so you can plan corrective work like a service and load upgrade.
