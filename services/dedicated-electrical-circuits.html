Overview

Dedicated Electrical Circuits for your home

Many appliances and pieces of equipment are designed to run on their own circuit. When high-demand equipment shares a circuit with other loads, the result can be tripped breakers, dimming lights, poor performance, or overheating.

Bright Flow Services installs new dedicated circuits for major appliances, hot tubs, saunas, and home workshops. We review each piece of equipment’s electrical requirements and your panel’s available capacity, then plan the circuit from the panel to the point of use.

What this service can include

  • Circuits for ranges, ovens, dryers, refrigerators, freezers, and other major appliances
  • Hot tub and spa circuits with the required disconnect and protection
  • Sauna circuits matched to the heater’s requirements
  • Workshop circuits for power tools, compressors, and equipment
  • 240-volt and 120-volt circuits as the equipment requires
  • A review of panel capacity before new circuits are added
Benefits

Why homeowners choose dedicated electrical circuits

Safer operation

Circuits sized for the load help prevent overheating and overloaded wiring.

Reliable performance

Equipment receives the power it was designed for, so it runs as intended.

Fewer tripped breakers

Separating heavy loads keeps everyday circuits from overloading.

Room to grow

Planning circuits now makes it easier to add equipment later.

FAQ

Dedicated Electrical Circuits questions

What is a dedicated circuit?

A circuit that serves a single appliance or piece of equipment rather than sharing capacity with outlets and lights elsewhere in your home.

Which appliances need a dedicated circuit?

Many major appliances, such as ranges, dryers, and water heaters, and often refrigerators, microwaves, and dishwashers, are installed on their own circuits. The manufacturer’s installation instructions and applicable codes determine the requirements for your specific equipment.

What does a hot tub need electrically?

Hot tubs require a dedicated circuit sized to the unit, along with a disconnect and ground-fault protection as required. Requirements vary by model, so we review your hot tub’s specifications before planning the installation.

Can my panel handle another circuit?

It depends on available space and capacity. We evaluate your panel first, and if it’s near its limit we’ll explain your options, which can include a panel upgrade.

Can I use a heavy-duty extension cord instead?

Extension cords aren’t a substitute for permanent wiring and can overheat under sustained loads. A dedicated circuit is the safer long-term solution for high-demand equipment.
